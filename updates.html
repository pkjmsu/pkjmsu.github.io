  • 2026 — Academic visit & international collaboration

    Visiting Research Scholar at Kasetsart University

    Prof. Prafulla K. Jha has been selected as a Visiting Research Scholar at Kasetsart University, Thailand, for 2026, strengthening collaborative research and academic exchange between CCMP Lab and Kasetsart University.

    About the visiting-scholar programme and eligibility

    The Faculty of Science's published 2025 Visiting Research Scholar (VRC) policy supports collaboration with researchers from international academic and research institutions. Its objectives include increasing international publications in SJR Q1/Q2 journals and strengthening the faculty's research impact.

    Who is eligible? Applications pair a current Faculty of Science academic or researcher with an international visiting researcher. The host must have at least five SJR-indexed papers in the preceding five years, including three as corresponding author. The visitor must have at least 20 Q1/Q2 papers over five years, including ten as corresponding author, or an h-index of at least 35. Concurrent visiting appointments or funding at other Thai institutions are excluded during the award.

  • 15–21 June 2026 — International school & research training

    Paras Patel participates in the 2026 EPW School

    CCMP Lab PhD student Paras Patel participated in the 2026 EPW School, held at the Abdus Salam International Centre for Theoretical Physics (ICTP), Trieste, Italy, from 15 to 21 June 2026 (seven days).

    The school combined theory lectures with practical training in first-principles electron–phonon and excited-state calculations. Topics included charge transport, optical response, and superconductivity, with tutorials using EPW and related electronic-structure software.

    Organisers and supporting organisations

    The organisers were Feliciano Giustino and Sabyasachi Tiwari (University of Texas at Austin), Roxana Margine (Binghamton University), Ralph Gebauer, Nicola Seriani and Adriana Pinto (ICTP), Emmanouil Kioupakis (University of Michigan, Ann Arbor), and Samuel Poncé (Université catholique de Louvain).

    The school was sponsored by ICTP, Psi-k, and the EPW Collaboration.

  • 1–12 June 2026 — International school & research training

    CCMP students attend the Joint ICTP–MARVEL College

    CCMP Lab PhD students Paras Patel, Saurav Patel, and Zarna Ponkiya attended the Joint ICTP MARVEL College: Materials simulations in the age of AI, held from 1 to 12 June 2026 at the Abdus Salam International Centre for Theoretical Physics (ICTP), Trieste, Italy, in the Budinich Lecture Hall.

  • November 2026 — Journal of Power Sources 692, 240949
    S. Mishra, R. Sainda, S. Mishra, D. Vinod, P. K. Jha, V. Kulshrestha, "Rewiring hydration topology enables polarization enhanced proton transport in PFSA membranes." DOI: 10.1016/j.jpowsour.2026.240949

    Highlight: A melamine–H3BTC hydrogen-bonded organic framework (1 wt%) reworks the hydration network of PFSA membranes, boosting proton conductivity to 9.4×10⁻² S cm⁻¹ at 30 °C and 1.0×10⁻¹ S cm⁻¹ at 80 °C.

    Research summary

    This study introduces a melamine–H3BTC hydrogen-bonded organic framework (HOF) to fundamentally reconfigure the hydration and proton-conduction network within Nafion/PFSA membranes. The optimized 1 wt% HOF membrane achieves proton conductivities of 9.4 × 10−2 S cm−1 at 30 °C and 1.0 × 10−1 S cm−1 at 80 °C, with reduced activation energy. DFT and MD simulations reveal strong interfacial binding, enhanced polarization, continuous hydrogen-bond pathways, and enhanced hydronium diffusion. The work identifies 1 wt% HOF as a critical loading, beyond which aggregation begins to disrupt proton-transport pathways. These findings establish hydrogen-bond topology engineering and interfacial polarization as key design principles for high-performance and durable proton-exchange membranes.

  • September 2026 — Materials Today Physics 67, 102198
    A. Gauswami, B. R. Dhori, P. K. Jha, "Valley polarization and symmetry-driven spin splitting in altermagnetic Fe2S2O and Janus Fe2SSeO monolayers and bilayers." DOI: 10.1016/j.mtphys.2026.102198

    Highlight: Robust valley polarization and symmetry-driven spin splitting persist in altermagnetic Fe2S2O and Janus Fe2SSeO monolayers and bilayers, with piezomagnetic coupling for magnetoelastic control.

    Research summary

    We present a first-principles study of altermagnetism in Fe2S2O and Janus Fe2SSeO monolayers and bilayers, revealing tunable valley polarization and piezomagnetism. The intrinsically symmetry-broken Fe2SSeO bilayer exhibits robust momentum-dependent spin splitting, highlighting its potential for 2D spintronic and valleytronic applications.

  • August 2026 — Small Methods, e70991
    A. Gauswami, P. K. Jha, "A Unified Review of Altermagnetism: Momentum-Space Spin Polarization and Emerging Applications." DOI: 10.1002/smtd.70991

    Highlight: A unified review of altermagnetism — a magnetic class combining zero net magnetization with momentum-dependent spin splitting — covering theory, materials and emerging device applications.

    Research summary

    Altermagnetism is a distinct class of collinear magnetism characterized by zero net magnetization but symmetry-driven, momentum-dependent spin splitting. This review covers its fundamental principles, symmetry, materials, experimental identification, computational approaches, and emerging applications in spintronics, valleytronics, magnonics, orbitronics, superconductivity, and quantum technologies.

  • July 2026 — Solar Energy 312, 114580
    N. Joshi, N. A. Tukadiya, A. Pandya, P. K. Jha, "Investigation on electronic, optical, thermal and photovoltaic properties of AgBiOS oxychalcogenide." DOI: 10.1016/j.solener.2026.114580

    Highlight: AgBiOS shows a direct band gap of 1.26 eV; an FTO/TiO2/AgBiOS/CuI device achieves a power conversion efficiency of 21.98%.

    Research summary

    This study combines DFT and SCAPS-1D to investigate AgBiOS as a lead-free solar-cell absorber. AgBiOS shows a 1.26 eV band gap, strong visible-light absorption, and a simulated power conversion efficiency of 21.98% in an FTO/TiO2/AgBiOS/CuI device. The results also reveal good defect tolerance and provide guidelines for efficient thin-film photovoltaic design.

  • June 2026 — Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics 28(24), 14540–14550
    R. Sainda, P. K. Jha, "Electric field-induced bond length and bandgap tunability for enhanced reactivity of nitride nanorings." DOI: 10.1039/d6cp01438k

    Highlight: Field-tunable semiconductor behaviour in Al9N9/B9N9 nitride nanorings, where an applied electric field elongates bonds and contracts the ring to boost chemical reactivity.

    Research summary

    This study presents a comprehensive DFT investigation of Al9N9 and B9N9 nanorings under an external electric field to control their structural and electronic properties. Increasing electric field induces bond elongation, ring contraction, charge redistribution, and increasing dipole moment, while Al9N9 retains its cyclic structure across the studied field range. Al9N9 exhibits a stable field-tunable response, whereas B9N9 undergoes significant structural distortion at higher fields. The study highlights Al9N9 as a robust electric-field-tunable semiconductor with potential applications in nanoelectronics, sensing, and field-controlled molecular devices.

  • June 2026 — 2D Materials 13(3), 032002
    H. T. Nair, Z. D. Ponkiya, D. Chodvadiya, P. K. Jha, B. Chakraborty, "Emerging frontiers of MXenes: structure, properties and energy applications." DOI: 10.1088/2053-1583/ae726b

    Highlight: A review of MXenes covering their structure, MAX-phase etching synthesis, and applications in the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) and energy storage.

    Research summary

    Since their discovery in 2011, MXenes have emerged as promising 2D materials with tunable mechanical, electronic, optical, and magnetic properties. This review provides an integrated overview of their fundamental properties, synthesis strategies, and emerging applications. MXenes are mainly synthesized from MAX phases through selective etching, while alternative methods enable control over surface chemistry and properties. Their properties can be tailored through defect engineering, doping, strain, and hybrid formation, enabling applications in optoelectronics, photocatalysis, spintronics, and energy storage. Recent advances in hydrogen evolution catalysis and electrochemical energy storage are highlighted, along with DFT-guided design principles and future prospects.

  • May 2026 — Physical Review B 113, 184108
    P. Patel, M. H. Dalsaniya, S. Patel, D. Kurzydłowski, K. J. Kurzydłowski, P. K. Jha, "Revisiting the high-pressure phase transitions of yttrium: Insights from density functional theory." DOI: 10.1103/jp7h-d7q3

    Highlight: r2SCAN reproduces the experimentally known transition pressures of yttrium (<30 GPa), predicts a new P-62c intermediate phase between the hcp and Sm-type structures, and reveals the s-to-d charge transfer driving the phase transitions.

    Research summary

    This work provides a first-principles understanding of the pressure-induced structural phase transitions in yttrium below 30 GPa. Using the r2SCAN functional, the experimentally observed transition pressures are accurately reproduced, while phonon and elastic analyses reveal vibrational and mechanical softening as key drivers of the transitions. A previously unidentified P̄62c intermediate phase is predicted between the hcp and Sm-type phases, highlighting the role of soft-mode dynamics in high-pressure structural transformations. The study further connects these transitions with pressure-induced s-to-d electronic charge transfer and evolving chemical bonding.

  • April 2026 — Inorganic Chemistry Frontiers 13(11), 4867–4897
    T. Dyrda-Terniuk, M. Ehlert, K. Roszak, G. Trykowski, K. Szpotkowski, Ł. Skowroński, O. Pryshchepa, R. van Eldik, K. Mizgalska, W. Guida, A. Karolak, R. Sainda, P. K. Jha, P. Pomastowski, "Lactoferrin as an active coordination scaffold for ruthenium (III)." DOI: 10.1039/d6qi00255b

    Highlight: Lactoferrin acts as a Langmuir-type active coordination scaffold for ruthenium (III).

    Research summary

    This work investigates the molecular mechanism of Ru(III) coordination with bovine lactoferrin (bLF) and its structural and biological consequences. It combines molecular docking, molecular dynamics, DFT, spectroscopy, microscopy, SAXS, and ICP-OES to establish the binding mechanism. Ru(III) binding follows a predominantly Langmuir-type, saturation-controlled process, with secondary Ru accumulation appearing at high loading. The work establishes lactoferrin as a promising protein-based coordination scaffold for Ru(III) delivery and biomedical applications.

  • January 2026 — Journal of Physics: Condensed Matter 38, 035501
    S. Patel, P. K. Jha, "Ferroelectric Rashba Topological Phase in Noncentrosymmetric CsSiBi." DOI: 10.1088/1361-648X/AE3511

    Highlight: A topological insulating phase coexisting with isotropic Rashba spin splitting in CsSiBi, with ferroelectric switching offering electric-field control of the spin topology.

    Research summary

    In this work, we explore first-principles approaches to uncover the interplay of symmetry, spin–orbit coupling, topology, and ferroelectricity in noncentrosymmetric quantum materials. CsSiBi is coined as an intrinsic composite quantum compound with a topological insulating phase, isotropic Rashba spin-splitting and ferroelectric switching with large polarization and a low switching barrier. Our study further highlights the importance of full-potential codes for reliably describing the Rashba effect. The confluence of the aforementioned properties opens avenues for next-generation multifunctional materials for spintronics and nanoelectronics.

  • January 2026 — Journal of Physics: Condensed Matter 38, 025802
    A. Gauswami, P. K. Jha, "Strain-controlled chiral magnons and spin-dependent Seebeck effect in tetragonal β-MnO2 altermagnet: a DFT study." DOI: 10.1088/1361-648X/ae3353

    Highlight: Strain-controlled chiral magnons with nonreciprocal dispersion and tunable spin splitting in a tetragonal β-MnO2 altermagnet, together with an orientational spin-dependent Seebeck effect.

    Research summary

    We present a first-principles study of chiral magnons and spin-dependent transport in tetragonal β-MnO2, revealing nonreciprocal magnon dispersion and tunable spin splitting under strain. These results establish β-MnO2 as a promising material for field-free spintronic and magnonic applications.

  • 2026 — Solar Energy
    "Machine learning and SCAPS-1D based prediction and validation of RbGeBr3 perovskite solar cell." DOI: 10.1016/j.solener.2025.113760

    Highlight: XGBoost predicts a power conversion efficiency of 30.67% for RbGeBr3 perovskite, validated by SCAPS-1D at 31.76%, demonstrating the potential of ML-assisted screening for high-efficiency perovskite solar cells.

    Research summary

    This study combines machine learning and SCAPS-1D to predict the photovoltaic performance of RbGeBr3 perovskite. XGBoost predicted a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 30.67%, while SCAPS-1D validation achieved 31.76%. The results demonstrate the potential of ML-assisted screening for designing high-efficiency perovskite solar cells.
